# Keep the Engine, Rethink the Product

**Verdict: yes, but conditionally — and the conditions change what the product is.** A deterministic, rule-based COBOL transpiler (a program that mechanically rewrites one language into another using fixed rules, so the same input always produces the same output) whose results are checked byte-for-byte against a real COBOL compiler is a genuinely rare and defensible asset in October 2026, because the whole market has just pivoted from "can AI translate COBOL?" to "can anyone *prove* the translation is right?" — and no vendor, large or small, publishes the kind of reproducible, compiler-checked evidence this project has built across 40 adversarial rounds, 574 oracle-verified programs and 241 root-caused fixes. But the product *as currently framed* does not make sense: Scala as the only output language has no demonstrated buyer demand and a shrinking talent base; the correctness oracle is GnuCOBOL (a free, open-source COBOL compiler) rather than IBM's compiler that actually runs the banks, and the two are documented to disagree in ways that matter for money; the reference-modification bug still returns placeholder values for a construct used in everyday COBOL; and the 105 commits of campaign work have never been merged into the main branch. Meanwhile the market this product would sell into is contracting at the exit end: Gartner predicted in June 2026 that more than 70% of mainframe-exit projects started this year will fail and 75% of exit vendors will pivot or disappear by 2030, AWS made its own deterministic COBOL-to-Java translation free in March 2026, and buyers are regulated institutions who buy multi-year programs from large integrators, not standalone tools. The honest conclusion is that the *verification engine and its corpus* are worth continuing, the *Scala-only conversion product* is not, and the next six months should be spent on four things in order: merge and stabilize what exists, fix the reference-modification gap, calibrate the oracle against IBM COBOL, and talk to ten potential channel partners before writing a single line of platform code.

## The 2026 market buys "modernize in place"; wholesale conversion is the minority path

The first thing a project owner needs to understand is that the people with mainframes mostly decided, in 2025–2026, not to leave them. Every available indicator points the same way. Gartner's June 18, 2026 press release predicted that **"more than 70% of mainframe exit projects initiated in 2026 will fail to produce the intended benefits due to an overestimation of generative AI tooling capabilities,"** and that **"by 2030, 75% of vendors operating in the mainframe exit market will pivot their business models or cease operations"** ([Gartner newsroom](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-06-18-gartner-predicts-more-than-70-percent-of-mainframe-exit-projects-will-fail-due-to-overestimation-of-generative-ais-capabilities)). The underlying paper, reported by The Register in April 2026, argued that "the sheer volume and interconnected complexity of this data make wholesale migration a physical and financial impossibility" for most large enterprises ([The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2026/04/15/gartner_mainframe_exit_analysis/)). Gartner's recommended alternative is to use AI "to enable modernization in place rather than accelerate migration off the platform" — in plain terms, keep the mainframe, understand and refactor the code where it sits, and wrap it in modern interfaces.

Buyer surveys agree, even the ones commissioned by vendors who sell migration. Kyndryl's September 2025 survey found **95% of organizations maintaining or increasing mainframe usage**, and although 98% were moving *some* applications off, the average share being moved fell from **36% to 28%** in a single year ([Kyndryl newsroom](https://www.kyndryl.com/us/en/about-us/news/2025/09/mainframe-modernization-delivers-big-roi)). BMC's 20th annual mainframe survey, fielded in spring 2025, found a record **97% of respondents view the mainframe as a long-term platform**, with 64% of new mainframe applications written in Java and 55% of existing applications being rewritten in Java — modernization *on* the platform, not off it ([SD Times](https://sdtimes.com/mainframe/mainframe-technology-shows-growth-and-change-in-20th-annual-bmc-survey/)). IBM's hardware numbers tell the same story from the supply side: IBM Z revenue rose 61–67% in Q4 2025 and 51% in Q1 2026 on the z17 cycle, described as IBM's strongest mainframe quarter in over 20 years ([DCD](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/ibm-mainframe-revenue-up-67-though-software-sales-disappoint/); [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/04/22/ibm-q1-earnings-report-2026.html)). Companies racing for the exits do not buy record quantities of new mainframes. The Q2 2026 drop of 42% was the normal end of a hardware cycle plus deal-timing issues, not a demand collapse ([SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2026/07/22/ibm-cuts-outlook-software-mainframe-weakness-weighs-results/)).

The money that *is* flowing goes to services, not tools. Kyndryl's 2026 financial-sector deals — Folksam, Banco Credibom, Broadridge, Velliv, Krungsri — are all "keep the mainframe, outsource or modernize around it" arrangements ([Kyndryl](https://www.kyndryl.com/us/en/about-us/news/2026/09/krungsri-core-banking-transformation)). US state unemployment-system replacements (Ohio's $83.3M Buckeye UI, New York's November 2026 cutover, Oregon's $106M system) are purchases of off-the-shelf vendor platforms, not code conversions ([GovTech](https://www.govtech.com/gov-experience/ohios-new-ui-system-will-come-with-a-83m-price-tag); [NYS DOL](https://dol.ny.gov/news/unemployment/announcement)). The most-publicized exit attempt, DOGE's March 2025 plan to rewrite the Social Security Administration's 60-million-line COBOL estate "in months," generated enormous criticism and a Warren–Wyden letter in June 2025, and then, as far as any accessible source shows, nothing: no completion, no formal cancellation, and a 2026 SSA modernization narrative about portal uptime rather than COBOL ([SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2025/03/28/doge-reportedly-planning-rewrite-social-security-administrations-software/); [Warren press page](https://www.warren.senate.gov/newsroom/news-coverage/senators-warn-doges-social-security-administration-work-could-break-benefits)). Dollar sizes for the "mainframe modernization market" exist only from low-tier syndicated-report firms and disagree by more than 2x (roughly $8.4B to $20.9B for 2026), so none should be put in a business plan ([Straits Research](https://straitsresearch.com/report/mainframe-modernization-market); [The Business Research Company](https://www.thebusinessresearchcompany.com/report/mainframe-modernization-global-market-report)).

What this means for the project: a business premised on *wholesale* COBOL-to-anything conversion is swimming against the analyst, buyer and vendor current of 2026. A business premised on *proving that a converted program behaves identically* — whether the destination is a Java service beside the mainframe, a Java program on the mainframe, or an off-platform rewrite — is swimming with it.

## AI tools made translation free and moved the fight to verification

The second thing to understand is what the large AI vendors actually did to this space in 2025–2026, because the common assumption — "LLMs can now translate COBOL, so a rule-based tool is obsolete" — is almost exactly backwards. An LLM (large language model) is a statistical text generator; it produces plausible output that may differ from run to run. What the big vendors shipped was LLMs for *understanding and documenting* COBOL, wrapped around deterministic engines for the *translation* step, with testing bolted on afterward.

IBM folded watsonx Code Assistant for Z into "IBM Bob," which reached general availability on April 29, 2026 and routes work to multiple models including Anthropic's Claude; the mainframe-specific "Bob Premium Package for Z" entered no-cost technical preview in mid-2026 and is the designated successor for COBOL-to-Java ([DevClass](https://www.devclass.com/development/2026/04/29/ibms-ai-coding-partner-bob-hits-general-availability/5219012); [IBM Bob blog](https://bob.ibm.com/blog/bob-for-z-announcement)). IBM's correctness story is the strongest of the LLM vendors: a published method that uses symbolic execution to generate COBOL unit tests and converts them into equivalent Java tests ([arXiv 2504.10548](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.10548v1)). But IBM's own quality paper concludes that LLM-translated code "cannot be trusted to correctly translate the original code" without validation, and IBM's execution oracle is a real z/OS machine, not GnuCOBOL ([arXiv 2507.23356](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.23356v1)).

AWS did the most consequential thing for this project's economics. AWS Transform for mainframe pairs agentic AI for analysis with a **deterministic** translation engine inherited from Blu Age, and in **March 2026 AWS made the code-transformation step free**, replacing the previous charge of about $0.103 per line of code ([AWS re:Post guide](https://repost.aws/articles/ARue7jnmK4RUSaQH0NkZ4wng/mainframe-modernization-with-aws-a-complete-guide-for-2026); [Qovery analysis](https://www.qovery.com/blog/aws-transform-what-it-does-and-what-it-costs)). AWS claims more than 400 COBOL modernization customers and over 1.1 billion lines analyzed, and its flagship outcome is Mercedes-Benz converting 1.3 million lines of COBOL to Java with a zero-incident go-live in under six months ([AWS blog](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/migration-and-modernization/reimagine-your-mainframe-applications-with-agentic-ai-and-aws-transform/); [AWS case study](https://aws.amazon.com/solutions/case-studies/mercedes-benz-transform-case-study/)). The lesson is blunt: **"we translate COBOL to Java deterministically" is now competing against a $0 product.** Any pitch must rest on something AWS does not deliver. Google's posture is similar — Gemini for analysis and rewrite, deterministic conversion through partners such as mLogica, and "Dual Run" (running old and new systems side by side on live traffic and comparing results) as the verification mechanism, though Dual Run remains in preview four years after its 2022 announcement ([Google Cloud docs](https://docs.cloud.google.com/mainframe-assessment-tool/docs/modernize-validate); [heise](https://www.heise.de/en/news/Google-Cloud-Mainframe-modernization-with-four-building-blocks-11399665.html)). Microsoft was named a Leader in Gartner's inaugural August 2026 Magic Quadrant for AI-augmented code modernization, but its COBOL story is an open-source sample agent framework whose correctness check is "it compiles" ([Azure blog](https://azure.microsoft.com/en-us/blog/microsoft-named-a-leader-in-the-2026-gartner-magic-quadrant-for-ai-augmented-code-modernization-tools/); [Azure-Samples README](https://github.com/Azure-Samples/Legacy-Modernization-Agents/blob/main/README.md)).

The general-purpose coding agents created the loudest moment and the least substance. Anthropic's February 23, 2026 blog post on Claude Code and COBOL wiped roughly 13% off IBM's share price in a day — IBM's worst session since 2000 — but the post's claims centered on discovery, analysis and documentation, and the selloff unwound within about three months ([The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2026/02/23/ibm_share_dive_anthropic_cobol/); [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/ibm-takes-lumps-2026-buy-132548771.html)). An independent test reported that Claude's analysis coverage of a program **ranged from 24% to 35% across attempts, varying by up to 42% between identical runs** ([TechChannel](https://techchannel.com/artificial-intelligence/claude-code-and-cobol/)). The April 2026 COBOL-Coder benchmark found that **general-purpose LLMs achieve near-zero performance on COBOL-specific tasks**, and even the best specialized model reached only about 84% functional correctness on curated COBOL-to-Java translation — far from the effectively 100% a rule-based transpiler must deliver on constructs it supports ([arXiv 2604.03986](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.03986)). A Bucknell study found deterministic orchestration matched LLM-controlled orchestration on accuracy while cutting variance and using up to 3.5x fewer tokens ([arXiv 2605.09894](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.09894)). The market handed down its own verdict: bloop, an LLM-first COBOL-to-Java startup, shut down on January 1, 2026, while the deterministic specialists (TSRI, Heirloom, Astadia/Amdocs, mLogica) all survived and collected ISG analyst leadership positions in 2026 by repositioning around "proof before cutover" ([bloop profile](https://bouncewatch.com/company/bloopai); [mLogica](https://mlogica.com/resources/blogs/mainframe-modernization-in-2026-from-conversion-to-proof)). mLogica's framing is the one to remember: "the real standard is no longer whether code can be translated, but whether the modernized system can be evidenced, governed, and safely put into production."

Non-determinism is a disqualifier for audit-grade migration, and determinism is this engine's core property. That is the good news. The bad news is that the translation step itself has been commoditized to zero by AWS and bundled into IBM's "stay" story, so the value has to be in the evidence, not the output.

## Nothing on the market matches this engine's evidence standard, but its oracle is not the mainframe

Here is where the project's distinctive claim holds up, and where it does not. Every vendor's public verification story is a sampling argument: Google and AWS compare data outputs of the whole system on recorded or live traffic, at field level with declared tolerances for timestamps and floating point ([Dual Run batch comparison](https://docs.cloud.google.com/mainframe-dual-run/docs/batch-comparison-overview?hl=en); [AWS Application Testing](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/m2/latest/userguide/concepts-apptest.html)); IBM generates unit tests per program with mocked external calls ([arXiv 2504.10548](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.10548v1)); the deterministic specialists rest on "correct by construction" plus customer regression tests and warranties ([TSRI](https://tsri.com/cobol-modernization/)). **No vendor or paper found combines a deterministic translator with an automated reference-compiler oracle that compiles and runs the original COBOL and diffs results byte-for-byte across an adversarially grown program corpus.** In compiler engineering this is standard practice — Csmith-style random program generation has found more than 400 bugs in C compilers — but no Csmith equivalent for COBOL exists, and the 2025–2026 academic adversaries (ACToR, MatchFixAgent, Locksmith) search the *input space of a fixed program*, not the *program space of the language* ([Regehr blog](https://blog.regehr.org/archives/510); [arXiv 2510.03879](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.03879v2); [arXiv 2607.28271](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.28271)). A 40-round LLM-driven program-space adversary with a compiler oracle is methodologically closer to compiler fuzzing than to anything the LLM-translation vendors do, and no COBOL instance of it was found in publication. Because testing consumes **more than 50% of migration project duration** by AWS's own estimate, a translator that ships with a permanent, machine-checkable equivalence corpus is aimed at the cost center buyers care about ([AWS What's New](https://aws.amazon.com/about-aws/whats-new/2025/12/transform-mainframe-testing-automation)).

The "but" is the oracle. GnuCOBOL passes about 98.3% of the NIST COBOL-85 conformance suite and offers `-std=ibm` and `-std=ibm-strict` dialect modes, but it explicitly "does not claim to be a 'Standard Conforming' implementation," runs natively in ASCII rather than the mainframe's EBCDIC character encoding, and its IBM mode is a best-effort approximation that the maintainers describe with the word "likely" ([GnuCOBOL](https://gnucobol.sourceforge.io/further-information.html); [GnuCOBOL forum](https://sourceforge.net/p/gnucobol/discussion/cobol/thread/2b404bea5f/)). The divergences are not academic. A 2016 migrant reported `COMPUTE NUM-C = ((NUM-A / 100) - (NUM-B / 100)) * 100` giving **100 on the IBM mainframe and 188 under GnuCOBOL**, because IBM sizes intermediate results from the receiving field while GnuCOBOL computes at full precision ([GnuCOBOL forum](https://sourceforge.net/p/gnucobol/discussion/cobol/thread/9a729f5c/)). An independent 2025–2026 project, ironwork, keeps a register of more than twenty places where `cobc -std=ibm-strict` and IBM Enterprise COBOL disagree, including ROUNDED intermediate handling, sign display of packed fields, and — critically — that **`-std=ibm-strict` sets binary truncation off (TRUNC(BIN)-like behavior) whereas IBM's default is TRUNC(STD)** ([ironwork dialect.md](https://github.com/Portll/ironwork/blob/main/docs/dialect.md)). Several divergences cut the dangerous way: GnuCOBOL is *more permissive* (misused INDEX variables work, EXTERNAL size mismatches only warn), so a program can pass a GnuCOBOL-based check while being wrong or crashing on z/OS ([GnuCOBOL forum](https://sourceforge.net/p/gnucobol/discussion/help/thread/1483e7dd1a/?limit=25)). The project has already found at least one GnuCOBOL-build-specific quirk itself (a bare numeric-literal CALL argument corrupted to blank), and its oracle is a 4.0 early-development build, not the 3.2 stable release.

In fairness, every open academic COBOL paper uses GnuCOBOL as its oracle for the same reason: it is the only freely runnable COBOL ([arXiv 2607.04092](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.04092)). "Matches GnuCOBOL" is therefore *comparable to the published state of the art*, and it is a far stronger evidentiary claim than the deterministic vendors publish. But it must never be marketed as "matches the mainframe." IBM COBOL for Linux on x86 exists as a commercial IBM-semantics compiler that runs on ordinary servers, and a calibration run through it (or a z/OS trial) is the single most important thing that would move the engine from "rare" to "credible to a bank" ([OSnews](https://www.osnews.com/story/133281/ibm-cobol-for-linux-on-x86-1-1-brings-cobol-capabilities-to-linux/)). The second is scope: byte-exact stdout of self-contained programs validates *language semantics*; it says nothing about CICS (IBM's transaction-processing system), DB2 or VSAM (IBM's database and indexed-file systems), JCL (the mainframe's job-control scripting) or EBCDIC data at rest, which is where practitioners say migrations actually die.

## Scala as the sole target is the project's largest strategic liability

The project's third premise — that Scala is a sensible destination language — is the weakest. Scala is a programming language that runs on the same Java Virtual Machine as Java; it is more concise and favored by some data-engineering and investment-banking teams, but it is a different language with a different, much smaller, talent pool. The evidence does not support it as the sole destination for banks, insurers and government.

Every volume indicator for Scala is small and flat-to-down. Stack Overflow's 2025 survey put extensive Scala use at **2.9% of developers**; JetBrains' 2025 survey put it at **2% as a primary language** ([Stack Overflow 2025](https://survey.stackoverflow.co/2025/technology/); [JetBrains](https://blog.jetbrains.com/research/2025/10/state-of-developer-ecosystem-2025/)). UK permanent job ads citing Scala fell from **0.72% to 0.29% of all IT ads** between 2024 and mid-2025 ([ITJobsWatch](https://www.itjobswatch.co.uk/jobs/uk/scala.do)). The Scala community's own 2026 survey drew **1,056 respondents versus 1,387 in 2023, a 24% drop**, and VirtusLab — the main commercial contractor on the Scala 3 compiler — wrote that Scala is "no longer as popular or fashionable as it once was" ([VirtusLab](https://virtuslab.com/blog/scala/our-impressions-from-the-scala-survey-2026)). The Scala Center cut its engineering team to **a single half-time employee** in 2025 ([Scala Center roadmap](https://scala.epfl.ch/records/2025-Q1-roadmap.html)). Akka, the flagship Scala framework, left open source for a per-core commercial license in 2022 ([ITPro](https://itpro.com/business/business-operations/369024/akka-to-move-away-from-open-source-licence-citing-funding-needs)), and Apache Flink removed its Scala API entirely in Flink 2.0 ([FLIP-265](https://cwiki.apache.org/confluence/spaces/FLINK/pages/231115782/FLIP-265+Deprecate+and+remove+Scala+API+support)). The counter-signals are real but thin: Scala 3.9 LTS shipped in September 2026, more than 92% of remaining teams are on Scala 3, and Scala developers hold the highest share of top-quartile salaries of any language ([Scala users forum](https://users.scala-lang.org/t/scala-3-9-0-lts-has-been-released/12372); [Scalac](https://scalac.io/state-of-scala-2025/)). That salary premium, though, is a scarcity premium — from a buyer's chair it is a *cost* of choosing the target, compounding over the 20–30-year life of a converted core system. **43% of Scala-using teams report difficulty hiring** ([Scalac](https://scalac.io/state-of-scala-2025/)).

On the demand side, the finding is stark: **every commercial COBOL-conversion vendor found emits Java or C#, and zero evidence of COBOL-to-Scala demand — no vendor, RFP, job post or conference talk — was found outside generic any-to-any AI converter web pages** ([Astadia](https://www.astadia.com/fact-sheets/codeturn-cobol-to-java); [codingfleet](https://codingfleet.com/code-converter/cobol/scala/)). The project's thesis that "no competitor targets Scala" is accurate, but the likeliest explanation is absence of demand, not an overlooked opportunity: a moat around a target nobody is asking for is not a moat. The "major banks use Scala" argument is partly true — Morgan Stanley's Optimus platform, Capital One and Deutsche Bank do run Scala — but their 2025–2026 job postings uniformly read "Java/Scala," treating Scala as one dialect inside a Java estate, and no bank was found announcing Scala *expansion* ([Scala Days 2023](https://scaladays.org/seattle-2023/supercharge-your-performance-with-the-optimus-cirrus-platform); [Morgan Stanley posting](https://www.themuse.com/jobs/morganstanley/java-scala-developer-1fd4e7)). Insurers and governments, two of the three target segments, show no Scala footprint in the evidence at all. Technically, modern Java has closed the gap: both languages bottom out in `java.math.BigDecimal` and byte arrays for COBOL's fixed-point decimals and record layouts, Java 21 added record patterns and pattern-matching switch, and the incumbent vendors already map COMP-3 (packed decimal, COBOL's compact money format) to BigDecimal in Java ([Inside.java](https://inside.java/2023/09/17/roadto21-pattern-matching/); [Micro Focus docs](https://microfocus.com/documentation/visual-cobol/vc90/DevHub/GUID-4F699B8D-9A5D-4EC3-A604-043822763571.html)). Scala's residual advantage is legibility of generated code; its residual cost is dependency surface (the Scala library, and Doobie — a functional-programming database library most bank Java teams do not run) and talent.

The uncomfortable corollary is that fixing this is a large job, not a small one. The generator has no target-neutral intermediate representation (an IR — an internal, language-independent description of the program that different output languages could be generated from). Roughly 21,600 lines of JavaScript across 11 files emit Scala text directly from the parsed COBOL, with Scala-specific syntax at some 848 sites, Scala type names leaking into the analysis layer, and SQL output specific to Doobie. The portable assets are the parser (about 11,000 lines), the runtime byte codecs (about 2,200 lines), and the entire 574-program oracle corpus and harness, whose design is target-neutral. The project's own market analysis already concedes the Scala-native buyer is "the ~dozen global banks with large existing Scala platforms … enough for first design partners, not for the whole company" and names Java as the planned mainstream target, but there is no written design for it. Every adversarial round that adds Scala-specific fixes to the expression generator makes a Java emitter more expensive; if Java is on the roadmap at all, the cheapest moment to introduce an IR is now.

## Practitioners say translation is the cheap part

The people who have actually done these migrations are consistent, and their view should calibrate expectations. Across the 2025 DOGE threads, the Hypercubic launch, the January 2026 "Ask HN: COBOL devs" thread and the February 2026 IBM-selloff threads on Hacker News, the dominant argument is that translating the code is the easy part; the expensive, failure-prone parts are undocumented business rules, regulated testing and sign-off, the surrounding platform, and the risk that a faithful machine translation is no more maintainable than the original. One practitioner recalled a migration where "only the tests would take a few thousand days," with regulators requiring human execution and sign-off of every test ([HN 47128907](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47128907)). Hypercubic's founders, pitching the opposite side, reported that modernization leaders told them projects fail "not because no one can parse COBOL, but because no one can answer 'why was this billing edge case added in 1995 and what breaks if we remove it'" ([HN 45877517](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45877517)). Working COBOL developers in January 2026 reported that AI output "struggled with strict COBOL formatting, drifting past column 72 or messing up period termination in nested IFs," and that compliance "is usually a hard stop" on sending code to external models at all ([HN 46678550](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46678550)). IBM's Rob Thomas and BMC said the same thing from the vendor side on the day of the selloff: "translating code itself isn't modernization" ([Seeking Alpha](https://seekingalpha.com/news/4555580-ibm-falls-most-since-2000-after-anthropic-cobol-ai-claims-thomas-says-translation-isnt-modernization)).

The failure modes practitioners describe in 2025–2026 are the ones this engine's corpus was built around: a translation tool that read a REDEFINES'd packed-decimal field "as an ordinary number" and "wrote corrupted binary into the database column," floating point sneaking into money math, and rounding modes not pinned to COBOL's ROUNDED ([Medium](https://medium.com/@ashutosh_veriprajna/the-cobol-migration-that-compiled-perfectly-and-still-corrupted-the-ledger-598aef136ac4)). Their stated acceptance bar is dual running "where both systems process the same input and the outputs are compared byte for byte until the differences are either eliminated or explained" — but on *production data over months*, at the logical-field level across EBCDIC/ASCII boundaries, not on synthetic programs under GnuCOBOL ([dev.to](https://dev.to/mecanik-dev/mainframe-migration-tools-what-works-and-what-fails-4k6l)). The two are often conflated in marketing and this project should not conflate them. The institutional mainframe community's 2026 messaging (SHARE Pittsburgh's z/COBOL sessions, the Open Mainframe Project's "Should I Stay or Should I Go" paper) is overwhelmingly modernize-in-place ([SHARE program](https://shares2026.conference-program.com/organization/?inst=1236272995583538733); [OMP white paper](https://openmainframeproject.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/14/2025/01/Mainframe-Programming-Languages-White-Paper.pdf)). The prediction, assembled from this record, is that practitioners would approve of the determinism and the compiler-based differential checking, then immediately challenge *which* compiler, *what* inputs, and *what scope* — and the project currently has good answers to none of those three.

## Where the project stands: done vs. pending

The following facts were verified directly from the repository (github.com/harshadasari/cobol-to-scala, branch `claude/analyze-codebase-pdPSZ`) and its git history as of October 5, 2026; nothing here comes from the web research.

**What has been done.** A 40-round adversarial-verification campaign ran against the conversion engine in two phases: rounds 1–14 completed on July 11, 2026 and were paused by the owner; rounds 15–40 resumed at the owner's request and completed July 21, 2026, with wrap-up closed July 23. In each round an AI "refuter" wrote 10–18 deliberately hostile COBOL programs, compiled and ran them under real GnuCOBOL to establish ground truth, converted them with the engine, ran the generated Scala, and compared standard output byte-for-byte; every genuine divergence was fixed at root cause and independently re-verified before commit. The campaign found and fixed **241 real bugs** (110 in rounds 1–14, 131 in rounds 15–40). The oracle-verified corpus grew from 48 programs at the start to 209 at round 14 to **574 today** (563 programs that run clean under GnuCOBOL plus 11 probes whose correct behavior is a compile rejection). There are **about 2,017 automated tests** (898 unit, 1,119 oracle-harness) with zero failures and 45 honest, individually documented `t.todo()` work-queue entries rather than silent passes. The capabilities built and verified are broad: byte-accurate record layouts and codecs (packed decimal, binary including COMP-5, zoned decimal with sign overpunch, EBCDIC cp037, IEEE-754 floating point); copybook expansion including COPY REPLACING and REPLACE; essentially the full procedure division (PERFORM/IF/EVALUATE/GO TO, all arithmetic verbs with correct ROUNDED, truncation and per-target ON SIZE ERROR, STRING/UNSTRING/INSPECT, SEARCH, SORT and MERGE via procedures); real file I/O for LINE SEQUENTIAL and RELATIVE files including REWRITE/DELETE/START, LINAGE, FILE STATUS codes and DECLARATIVES; recursive programs with correct BY REFERENCE/CONTENT semantics; and multi-program CALL interop. The RELATIVE-file storage model was fully rewritten in round 29 after the campaign discovered the original silently corrupted binary data containing a newline byte — the single most serious finding, and exactly the class of bug that only byte-level differential testing catches. Adjacent generators exist for EXEC SQL (to Doobie, compile-verified standalone but not wired into the main output), JCL structure and dataset lineage, and an honest CICS/BMS service skeleton that is explicitly not a behavioral conversion. The campaign's convergence bar (0–2 findings for two consecutive rounds) was met once, at rounds 36–37, after which rounds 38–40 deliberately broadened the search and found 6, 7 and 8 bugs including two entirely unimplemented statements — so the engine is demonstrably *not* adversarially exhausted. The wrap-up included an independent completeness audit (which found and fixed two ledger inconsistencies and a corpus-count drift), a truth pass over every docs file, a closing report (`docs/PROGRESS_STATUS.md`), repository cleanup and a published visual walkthrough. All of this is committed and pushed — and **sits on a branch 105 commits ahead of `main` that has never been merged**; the only pull request on the repository is #1 (February 18, 2026, trivial early scaffolding).

**What is pending.** The engine gaps, in risk order, start with **reference modification** — the `field(start:length)` substring syntax used in everyday COBOL — which still returns a placeholder instead of the real value almost everywhere and is the single largest remaining silent-wrong-output risk. **ORGANIZATION IS INDEXED** (VSAM-style keyed files, the dominant file type on real mainframes) is unimplemented and cannot be verified in the current sandbox because the installed GnuCOBOL build has indexed support compiled out. CALL BY REFERENCE/CONTENT of a group containing an OCCURS table into an ordinary subprogram silently passes empty data. GO TO/PERFORM escaping an active THRU range, UNSTRING with a data-name delimiter, SORT USING/GIVING and MERGE GIVING, ALTER, REDEFINES of a group with OCCURS, variable-length OCCURS DEPENDING ON, and USE FOR DEBUGGING are stubs, no-ops or fixed-max approximations, and EBCDIC numeric round-trip is not generator-verified. SQL generation is not wired into the main output; CICS is skeleton-only; IMS DL/I is not handled; JCL flow diagrams and build-pipeline skeletons are not generated. **The platform layer has not been started at all**: no authentication, CI/CD, containerization, observability, compliance or hardened UI/API — these, not the engine, are now the production blockers. Scala 3 is the only target, with no Java emitter and no IR. The correctness oracle is GnuCOBOL 4.0-early-dev, not IBM Enterprise COBOL, and no calibration against IBM COBOL has been done. Verification scope is byte-exact stdout of self-contained programs; there is no database, VSAM, CICS or JCL system-level parity testing. One stale, likely non-compiling fixture (`tests/output/TransProc.scala`, duplicate filler names, zero record lengths) should be removed before any outside reviewer sees the repository. And the merge to `main` has not happened.

Weighed into the verdict, these facts cut both ways. The campaign is the most rigorous thing in the project and is more evidence than most commercial vendors publish; 241 fixes also means the engine *had* 241 real bugs, and the trend line (an average of roughly five per round in the back half) says a 41st round would find more. A reader should hold both thoughts at once: this is a serious, honestly-documented engine, and it is not finished.

## Recommended next steps, in order

The ordering matters: the cheap, risk-reducing steps come first, the demand test comes before the expensive build, and the Java decision is made deliberately rather than by default.

A demo that hits it destroys the credibility the campaign built. | Oracle-verified corpus programs exercising `field(start:length)` pass byte-for-byte. |

Do not, in this period, build the platform layer (authentication, CI/CD, containers, UI) beyond what step 4's partners need to run the harness. Do not run a 41st adversarial round for its own sake until reference modification is fixed and the IBM calibration has told you which classes of divergence to hunt. Do not wire EXEC SQL or build CICS behavioral conversion until a partner asks for them; practitioners are right that these are where migrations die, but they are also where the hyperscalers and SIs have runtimes and teams, and a small engine should sell into that layer, not compete with it.

## Confidence and gaps in this research

The owner asked for a reality check, so the limits of this one should be plain. The researchers' direct web fetches were blocked for nearly every primary domain — Gartner, IBM, AWS, Google Cloud, arXiv, The Register, CNBC, SEC filings, the Scala Center and most vendor sites — and the shared search budget ran out before all follow-ups could be made. As a result, most figures above rest on search-engine snippets of the cited pages rather than a full read of those pages. Where two snippets disagreed (IBM's Q4 2025 Z growth of 61% vs 67%; the February 2026 IBM market-cap loss of $30B vs $40B) both are reported. **Reddit was not retrievable at all**, so the practitioner-sentiment picture rests on Hacker News snippets (comment bodies and usernames mostly unavailable), GnuCOBOL forum threads (read in full), GitHub material (read in full) and individual blog posts; r/cobol, r/mainframe, r/scala and r/java are a real hole. Vendor numbers (AWS's 400 customers and 1.1B lines, IBM's "10x," Toyota's 40M lines, Mercedes-Benz's zero-incident go-live) are self-reported and unaudited. The Gartner press-release text was confirmed across several independent outlets but the underlying paper is paywalled. The status of the DOGE/SSA COBOL rewrite after mid-2025 is simply undocumented in accessible sources; "no evidence of execution" is an inference, not a finding. No tier-one analyst dollar size for the conversion-tooling segment exists, and the syndicated figures should not be used. On Scala, the only hard job-trend series found is UK-only (ITJobsWatch); no US or LinkedIn series surfaced, and no 2026 RedMonk or Stack Overflow figure was found. On the oracle question, the richest GnuCOBOL-vs-IBM divergence threads are from 2016–2023, so whether GnuCOBOL 3.2 or 3.3 closed any of them is unverified; the ironwork register is a single independent project's work. Private verification practice at Blu Age, Heirloom, TSRI and mLogica may well include compiler-differential harnesses they do not publish — absence of publication is not absence of practice. Finally, no practitioner was found commenting directly on a "deterministic, compiler-verified" pitch; the predicted reactions in this report are assembled from what practitioners said about adjacent pitches, not observed. Overall confidence: high on the direction of the market and the Scala conclusion (many independent sources agree), high on the project-status facts (verified from the repository), medium on specific vendor figures, and low-to-medium on practitioner sentiment because of the Reddit gap.

## Conclusion

The question the owner asked — does a COBOL-to-Scala conversion engine still make sense? — turns out to have a different shape than it looks. The 2025–2026 upheaval did not make deterministic translators obsolete; it made *translation* free and *proof* scarce, and in doing so it moved the value of this project from the Scala emitter, which nobody asked for, to the 574-program compiler-checked corpus and the adversarial method that built it, which nobody else has published. The three words in the product name carry very different weight in October 2026: "COBOL" is a durable, shrinking-at-the-edges estate that will be modernized in place for decades; "conversion" is a commodity the hyperscalers give away; "Scala" is a liability. "Verified" — the word that is not in the name — is the one buyers, analysts and the surviving specialist vendors are all now paying for.

Going forward, the project's real competition is not Claude Code or IBM Bob but the absence of an IBM-calibrated oracle and the absence of a channel. Fix the first with a bounded calibration run and an honest divergence register; test the second with ten conversations before building anything that needs a login screen. If both come back positive, the Java-versus-Scala decision will largely make itself, and the campaign's rigor becomes a differentiated component inside someone else's multi-year modernization program — which is, by every indicator found here, where the money in this market will be through 2030. If they come back negative, the owner will have learned that at the cost of a few months rather than a platform build, and will still own the most carefully evidenced open COBOL equivalence corpus anyone has described in public.
